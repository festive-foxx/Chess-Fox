Chess Rules & Terminology Guide

How Chess Pieces Move

Pawn

Moves forward 1 square (or 2 squares on its very first move). Captures 1 square diagonally forward.

Knight

Moves in an "L" shape (2 squares in one direction, 1 square perpendicular). The only piece that can jump over others.

Bishop

Moves diagonally any number of open squares. Stays on the same light or dark square color throughout the match.

Rook

Moves horizontally or vertically across any number of unoccupied squares. Essential for castling and endgames.

Queen

Combines the power of a Rook and Bishop! Moves horizontally, vertically, or diagonally any number of open squares.

King

Moves 1 square in any direction. The most important piece—protect your King at all costs to prevent checkmate!

Essential Chess Terminology

Check

When a King is directly threatened with attack by an opponent piece. The player must get out of check immediately.

Checkmate

The King is under check and has no legal moves to escape. This immediately ends the match with a win!

Stalemate (Draw)

A player whose turn it is has no legal moves left, but their King is NOT in check. The game ends in a draw (tie).

Castling

A special move allowing the King to move 2 squares toward a Rook, and the Rook hops over. Only allowed if neither piece has moved yet and no squares in between are threatened.

En Passant

A special pawn capture. If an opponent advances a pawn 2 squares forward past your pawn, you can capture it diagonally on your very next move as if it had only moved 1 square.

Pawn Promotion

When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board (8th rank), it can immediately transform into a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight!
